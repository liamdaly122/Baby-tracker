# Expectant Father's Preparation Inventory (UK / Leeds-led, with US notes)

Author: Liam Daly
Research date: 26 October 2026. Statutory figures are 2026/27 tax-year values. Where a figure comes from a secondary source rather than GOV.UK/NHS, it is flagged.

This report is the content source for the pre-launch checklists, briefings and skills training in the Mission Control app. Every item should be carried into the app with its source URL as provenance.

## PART A: MASTER TIMELINE (what to do, when)

### As soon as pregnancy is confirmed (weeks 4 to 8)
- Book the **booking appointment** (NHS: 8 to 12 weeks; about 1 hour; midwife takes history, BP, urine, bloods including blood group; sickle cell/thalassaemia screening ideally before 10 weeks). Partner should attend if possible.
- Ask the midwife/GP to apply for the **maternity exemption certificate** (free prescriptions and NHS dental for mum until 12 months after due date/birth; backdated one month). Gotcha: don't claim free prescriptions before the certificate arrives, penalty up to £100.
- Start **folic acid / vitamin D** reminders for her; take over cooking strong-smelling food, keep dry crackers by the bed (morning sickness peaks about 6 to 12 weeks).
- Check **Healthy Start** eligibility (available from 10+ weeks on qualifying benefits; prepaid card, £4.25/week during pregnancy, more per child after).
- Open a shared **baby budget** and start a "baby fund" (first-year costs about £8,000 to £10,000 excluding childcare and lost income, see Part B2).
- Quietly check your **employer's paternity/enhanced leave policy** and whether it exceeds statutory. Don't need to tell work yet.
- Agree **who you tell and when** (many wait for the 12-week scan).

### 12 weeks (dating scan, 11 to 14 weeks)
- Attend the **dating scan** (confirms due date, number of babies, offers combined screening for Down's/Edwards'/Patau's; needs CRL 45 to 84 mm, i.e. 11+2 to 14+1 weeks). Your right to **unpaid time off for 2 antenatal appointments (max 6.5 hrs each including travel)** starts here. Use it wisely (dating scan + anomaly scan are the usual choices).
- Announce if you wish; take the "first scan photo" milestone.
- Start the **names shortlist**.
- Consider **sex and intimacy** reassurance: NHS says sex is safe in a normal pregnancy unless told otherwise (see B10).

### 16 weeks
- Midwife appointment (results of screening, BP, urine).
- **Whooping cough vaccine** can be given from 16 weeks (usually about 20; best before 32). Flu jab if Sept to Mar.
- Movements may start to be felt 16 to 24 weeks.

### 20 weeks (anomaly scan, 18 to 21 weeks)
- Attend the **20-week screening scan** (about 30 minutes; checks 11 physical conditions; may reveal sex if you want to know).
- Mum's employer must be told by the **15th week before the due date** (about week 25). Help her plan that conversation and the MATB1 form.
- Research **where to give birth** (home / midwife-led unit / labour ward) and start a draft birth plan.
- Research **antenatal classes**. NHS classes are free; book Leeds "Preparation for Birth and Beyond" from about 24 weeks; NCT courses fill up early, book now for a slot at about 30 to 34 weeks.

### 24 to 25 weeks
- **Your paternity-leave notice deadline is the 15th week before the EWC (expected week of childbirth)**, roughly week 25. Tell your employer the due date, that you want paternity leave, and 1 or 2 weeks (online form, formerly SC3). This is the single most-missed admin deadline for dads.
- If taking **Shared Parental Leave**, start the maths now (notice is 8 weeks per block; mum must curtail maternity leave).
- Self-employed mum: **Maternity Allowance** claim (form MA1) opens at 26 weeks.
- GTT (glucose tolerance test) at 24 to 28 weeks if she has risk factors (BMI 30 or more, previous big baby 4.5 kg or more, family history, South Asian/Black/Middle Eastern heritage, previous GDM).
- Mum's 25-week appointment (first pregnancy only).
- Start tracking movements as "her normal". No set number of kicks.

### 28 weeks
- **28-week appointment**: bloods (iron/anaemia, antibodies), anti-D if rhesus negative, BP, urine, bump measurement; discussion of labour, birth and newborn screening.
- **RSV vaccine** offered from 28 weeks (protects baby for first 6 months from bronchiolitis). Whooping cough if not yet done.
- Third trimester starts: nursery/sleep space, pram, car seat decisions. Order anything with lead times.
- Life insurance / will review (appoint guardians).
- Sure Start Maternity Grant (£500, on qualifying benefits, first baby) claim window opens 11 weeks before due date (about week 29).

### 32 weeks
- Antenatal classes typically running (NHS: 8 to 10 weeks before birth, weekly about 2 hrs).
- Start packing the **hospital bags** (some trusts suggest 32; have them done by 36).
- **Fit the car seat** and practise; get it checked (Halfords/retailer/council fitting events).
- Birth plan draft 2; discuss pain relief preferences and what you'll do if plans change.
- Plan **route to hospital + alternative**, parking, who looks after other kids/pets.
- Batch-cook and freeze meals. Deep clean before 36 weeks when she won't be able to.
- Dog/cat preparation (gates, baby noises, scents).

### 34 weeks
- Midwife appointment. Mum's maternity leave can start any time from 11 weeks before due date (week 29); most go at 36 to 38.
- Confirm **28-day notice** of exact paternity leave start if you're giving a fixed date (most choose "the day of birth" or "the week after birth", which doesn't require a date).
- Finalise names shortlist; agree the "visitors policy" with family.

### 36 weeks
- Midwife checks **baby's position**. Birth planning discussion.
- Hospital bags (hers, baby's, yours) **packed and in the car/hallway**. Car seat fitted. Fuel/charge in car. Change/card for parking. Numbers saved.
- Build the cot/Moses basket; wash and air baby clothes and bedding; set up changing station; sterilise bottles if bottle-feeding.
- Learn **nappy, bath, swaddle, wind, formula-prep** skills (classes, DadPad, Baby Buddy videos).
- Mum should do a **practice run** of what she does if labour starts when alone.

### 37+ weeks: "any day now"
- Baby is term. Stay within about 1 hour of home; keep phone on loud; tell work you may leave at short notice.
- Know the triggers to **call the Maternity Assessment Centre** (LGI 0113 392 6731 / St James's 0113 206 5781, 24 hrs): contractions every 5 minutes, waters breaking, bleeding, reduced movements, worried. Call 999 if she has an urge to push, you can see the head/cord, heavy bleeding, seizure.
- 38 and 40-week appointments; membrane sweep offered from about 39 to 40 weeks; at 41 weeks induction options are discussed.
- Keep the freezer stocked; tell visitors the plan; put the "do not disturb" on group chats.

### Birth day and first 2 weeks post-birth
- Day 0: skin-to-skin, delayed cord clamping (1 min or more, NICE), vitamin K, oxytocin injection offer, baby weighed/measured, wristbands. You can stay overnight at Leeds (bedside chair). Paternity leave activates.
- Within 72 hrs: **NIPE** newborn physical exam. Hearing screen before discharge or by 4 to 5 weeks.
- Within 36 hrs of coming home: first **community midwife visit**. About day 3: jaundice/weight check. **Day 5: heel-prick blood spot**. Day 10 to 14: midwife discharge, **health visitor new-birth visit** (7 to 14 days).
- Register the birth (42 days; Leeds Register Office, Merrion House, LS2 8BB, by appointment). **Both of you attend** if unmarried so you're on the certificate and get parental responsibility.
- Claim **Child Benefit** 48 hours after registering (backdated max 3 months; £27.05/week first child).
- Register baby with the **GP** (form; red book helps).
- Watch for maternal red flags (bleeding, headache, sepsis) and baby red flags (jaundice under 24 hrs, floppy, fever, no wet nappy 8 hrs).
- Baby blues normal in first 2 weeks; beyond that or worsening means PND, see GP/HV.
- Add baby to life insurance/wills; consider 30-hours childcare application from 23 weeks old.
- Week 6 to 8: mum's GP postnatal check; baby's 6 to 8 week check and first vaccines (8 weeks).

## PART B: DETAILED INVENTORY BY TOPIC

### B1. Admin and legal

**Paternity Leave (England, Scotland, Wales; NI devolved)**
- What: up to **2 weeks** leave, as one block or **two separate 1-week blocks**, any time within **52 weeks** of birth; cannot start before birth. A "week" = your normal working week. Source: https://www.gov.uk/paternity-pay-leave/leave
- Eligibility for *leave*: employee; father/partner of mother/adopter/intended parent; **day-one right since 6 April 2026** (Employment Rights Act 2025 removed the 26-week service rule). Source: https://www.gov.uk/paternity-pay-leave/eligibility ; https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/employment-rights-act-2025-changes-from-6-april-2026
- Also from 6 April 2026: you can take paternity leave **after** Shared Parental Leave for the same child (previously barred). Source: https://www.dacbeachcroft.com/en/what-we-think/family-leave-changes-taking-effect-in-april-2026
- Notice: tell employer the **due date and intention to take leave by the 15th week before the EWC** (online form, formerly SC3; employer's own form OK), then **28 days' notice** of start date and whether 1 or 2 weeks; 28 days to change dates. No proof of pregnancy needed. Source: https://www.gov.uk/paternity-pay-leave/how-to-claim
- Transitional rule: babies due 5 April to 25 July 2026 only needed 28 days' notice if newly eligible; now expired; babies due from 26 July 2026 follow the normal 15-week rule.
- Gotcha: the 15-week deadline lands at about 25 weeks of pregnancy, before most dads have thought about it. Put it in the app's timeline with a nag.

**Statutory Paternity Pay**
- **£194.32/week or 90% of average weekly earnings, whichever is lower** (from 6 April 2026; 2025/26 was £187.18). Paid via payroll, taxed/NI'd. Source: https://www.gov.uk/paternity-pay-leave/pay
- Eligibility for *pay* still requires **26 weeks' continuous employment by the end of the qualifying week** (15th week before EWC), employment up to the birth, and earnings **of £129/week or more**. Day-one right applies to leave, **not pay**. Source: https://www.gov.uk/paternity-pay-leave/eligibility
- Gotcha: many employers pay enhanced paternity pay only if you give notice correctly. Check the policy.

**Shared Parental Leave (SPL) and Pay (ShPP)**
- Share up to **50 weeks leave and 37 weeks pay** (mum curtails her 52/39). Up to **3 blocks each**, more if employer agrees; **8 weeks' notice** before each block; **20 SPLIT days** each (in addition to mum's 10 KIT days). Source: https://www.gov.uk/shared-parental-leave-and-pay ; https://www.gov.uk/shared-parental-leave-and-pay/booking-blocks-of-leave
- Eligibility (Acas summary): the parent taking SPL needs 26 weeks with the same employer by the 15th week before EWC; the other parent needs 26 weeks' work in the 66 weeks before EWC earning £30/week or more in 13 of them; self-employed cannot take SPL/ShPP themselves. ShPP is at the statutory rate. Source: https://www.acas.org.uk/shared-parental-leave-and-pay/checking-eligibility-for-shared-parental-leave
- Gotcha: SPL take-up is low because ShPP is flat-rate; model the household income hit before committing.

**Unpaid Parental Leave**
- 18 weeks per child up to 18th birthday, max 4 weeks/year, whole weeks; day-one right since 6 April 2026 (previously 1 year). Notice 21 days (GOV.UK notice page). Source: https://www.gov.uk/parental-leave/entitlement ; https://ifamagazine.com/day-one-rights-to-paternity-and-unpaid-parental-leave-what-changes-are-occurring-on-6-april-2026/

**Time off for antenatal appointments (partner)**
- Unpaid, **2 appointments, max 6.5 hours each including travel**; employer may ask for a declaration but not evidence; can agree to use holiday instead. Source: https://www.gov.uk/working-when-pregnant-your-rights ; https://www.acas.org.uk/pregnancy-at-work/antenatal-appointments
- Mum: *paid* time off for all antenatal care, including classes recommended by a midwife.

**Mum's maternity rights (so you can support the admin)**
- 52 weeks SML (26 OML + 26 AML); earliest start 11 weeks before EWC; compulsory 2 weeks post-birth (4 if factory); auto-starts if off sick with pregnancy illness in last 4 weeks; 8 weeks' notice to change return date; **10 KIT days**; must tell employer by 15th week before EWC; employer must do a risk assessment. Source: https://www.gov.uk/maternity-pay-leave/leave ; https://www.gov.uk/employee-rights-when-on-leave
- Redundancy protection now runs from start of leave to **18 months after birth**. Source: https://www.gov.uk/employee-rights-when-on-leave
- **Maternity Allowance** (self-employed / not eligible for SMP): £194.32 or 90% AWE for 39 weeks, tax-free; claim on MA1 from 26 weeks. Source (secondary): https://www.workingfamilies.org.uk/articles/maternity-allowance

**Registering the birth**
- **Within 42 days** (England, Wales, NI; Scotland 21 days via NRS). Register in the district where baby was born (or any office, which forwards details); some hospitals offer on-site registration. Bring photo ID and the red book. Registration is free; certificates are charged. Source: https://www.gov.uk/register-birth
- Leeds: appointment-only, **Leeds Register Office, Merrion House, Merrion Way, LS2 8BB**, Mon to Fri 8:45 to 15:55 (some Saturdays); book online; phone 0113 222 4408. Source: https://www.leeds.gov.uk/births-deaths-and-marriages/register-a-birth
- Gotcha: if you're **not married**, the father's details only go on the register if **both attend** (or via a statutory declaration of parentage). Mum alone = no dad on the certificate = no automatic parental responsibility.

**Parental responsibility (unmarried fathers)**
- Mother has it automatically. Father has it if married/civil-partnered to mother at birth (or marries later), or by **jointly registering the birth** (England and Wales, births since 1 Dec 2003). Otherwise: parental responsibility agreement with the mother, or court order. Source: https://www.gov.uk/parental-rights-responsibilities/who-has-parental-responsibility

**Child Benefit**
- **£27.05/week eldest, £17.90 each additional** (from 6 April 2026). Claim **48 hours after registering**; backdate up to **3 months**. Need birth certificate (can claim without, slower), bank details, both NI numbers. Only one person claims; claimant gets NI credits toward state pension, so the **lower earner/non-working parent should claim**. **High Income Child Benefit Charge**: taper from £60,000 to £80,000 individual adjusted net income; you can **claim but opt out of payments** to keep NI credits and the child's NI number. Source: https://www.gov.uk/child-benefit/how-to-claim ; https://www.gov.uk/child-benefit/what-youll-get ; https://www.which.co.uk/money/tax/tax-credits-and-benefits/child-benefit-ajRMh2r8Efbr
- Gotcha: forgetting to claim at all because of HICBC loses NI credits and delays the child's NI number.

**Wills, life insurance, guardians**
- Without a will, courts/local authority decide who raises the child if both die; a will lets you **appoint guardians**. Term life insurance covering mortgage + income replacement (rule of thumb 10x salary) + childcare to 18; write policies **in trust**. Some insurers offer free parent cover (e.g. Aviva £15k per parent per child under 4). Sources: https://trustedtraders.which.co.uk/money/wills-and-probate/passing-on-your-money/will-writing-for-new-parents-aFSAK9n8ctbN ; https://www.aviva.co.uk/insurance/life-products/life-insurance/knowledge-centre/family-protection-to-do-list/ ; https://pocketwise.co.uk/personal-finance/family-costs/financial-checklist-new-parents-uk/

**Telling work (how)**
- Put it in writing (email) with the due date, that you intend to take paternity leave, 1 or 2 weeks, and (if known) when. Ask about enhanced pay, flexible working on return, and whether they'll honour "day of birth" start. Agree a handover plan 4 weeks out.

**US equivalents (brief)**
- **FMLA**: 12 workweeks unpaid, job-protected, for birth/bonding within 12 months of birth; eligibility = 12 months' service, 1,250 hours, employer with 50+ employees within 75 miles; 30 days' notice when foreseeable; fathers have identical rights; intermittent bonding leave needs employer approval. Source: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fmla/faq
- Paid family leave exists only in some states (CA, NY, NJ, RI, WA, MA, CT, DC, OR, CO etc.), e.g. CA 60 to 70% pay up to 8 weeks; WA up to 12 weeks at up to 90%. Average US paternity leave actually taken is under a week. Source: https://www.fatherly.com/love-money/paternity-leave-laws-state-us/
- No birth-registration deadline equivalent of 42 days; hospitals file the birth certificate and SSN application.

### B2. Money

- **First-year cost estimates (UK)**: about £8,460 (Smart Cells, 2026, via LV); about £9,113 including one-off purchases (Babies and Children, via Nuby); range £6,000 to £11,000+. Childcare dominates thereafter: full-time under-2 nursery about £15,000/yr (Coram, via YBS). Sources: https://www.lv.com/about-us/press/cost-of-a-child-2016 ; https://uk.nuby.com/blogs/real-talk/how-much-does-it-cost-to-raise-a-baby-nuby-uk ; https://ybs.co.uk/guides/life-moments/how-much-does-it-cost-to-raise-a-child ; https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/blog/life-events/what-is-the-average-cost-to-have-a-baby
- **Buy new**: car seat (unknown crash history is invisible; RoSPA: only accept from a family member/friend you trust), cot mattress (firm, flat, waterproof; Lullaby Trust), breast pump flanges/teats. **Fine second-hand/borrowed**: pram/travel system frame, Moses basket (new mattress), bouncer, baby bath, sling, clothes (babies outgrow first size in about 3 to 4 weeks), toys, books, steriliser. Sources: https://childcarseats.org.uk/choosing-using/second-hand-child-seats/ ; https://rospa.com/Home-Safety/Advice/Product/Second-hand-Goods
- **Benefits and grants**: Child Benefit (above); **Sure Start Maternity Grant** £500 one-off, England/Wales/NI, on qualifying benefits, usually first child only, claim 11 weeks before due date to 6 months after (Scotland: Best Start Grant); **Healthy Start** card from 10 weeks pregnant on qualifying benefits (£4.25/week pregnancy); **Maternity Allowance** if no SMP; Universal Credit child element. Sources: https://www.turn2us.org.uk/get-support/information-for-your-situation/sure-start-maternity-grant ; https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/family/maternity-grants-pregnancy-benefits/
- **Childcare funding**: 30 hours/week funded childcare for working parents from the term after baby turns **9 months**; each parent earning £195/week or more, neither over £100k; **apply from when the child is 23 weeks old**, reconfirm every 3 months; also Tax-Free Childcare (20% top-up). Source: https://leeds.gov.uk/children-and-families/early-years/funded-childcare-9-months-4-years ; https://moneytothemasses.com/news/free-childcare-for-2-year-olds-how-to-apply
- Gotcha: budget for the **income drop** during maternity leave (SMP is 90% for 6 weeks then flat rate). That's the real cost, not the pram.

### B4. Antenatal classes

- **NHS classes**: free, start 8 to 10 weeks before due date, weekly about 2 hrs; cover labour, pain relief, interventions, newborn care, feeding, emotional wellbeing; partner welcome. Leeds: **Preparation for Birth and Beyond**, free, 4 weekly sessions + reunion, online or at children's centres, includes a birth-partner-role session; **book from about 24 weeks** via Eventbrite. Also "Meet the Midwives" drop-in at Leeds Baby Bank (Armley), open to dads. - **NCT**: paid; **Essentials** (about 12 hours, cheaper) and **Signature** (longer, about 2/3 on labour and birth, small group); price covers both of you; typical £200 to £300 depending on area/hours; some NHS-commissioned free places. Source: https://www.nct.org.uk/courses-workshops/nct-antenatal-course/frequently-asked-questions
- **Hypnobirthing**: breathing/relaxation/self-hypnosis; partners learn massage and breathing; about £140 to £250 per couple (KGH course £149; some NHS trusts run paid courses); NHS: can be used alongside pain relief, no guarantee of fewer interventions. Sources: https://yorkhospitals.nhs.uk/seecmsfile/?id=1968 ; https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/preparing-for-the-birth/antenatal-classes/

### B5. Birth plan

NHS says it's a preference list, not a contract; "things may change at the last minute." Downloadable template exists. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/preparing-for-the-birth/how-to-make-a-birth-plan/ ; https://www.nhs.uk/start-for-life/pregnancy/preparing-for-labour-and-birth/what-to-include-in-your-birth-plan/

Contents to decide together:
- **Where**: home (first baby: serious outcome 9/1000 vs 5/1000 in hospital; second+ as safe as hospital; no epidural), **alongside midwife-led unit** (lower forceps/ventouse rates, obstetric care on site), **freestanding MLU** (no epidural, transfer if complications), **labour ward** (epidural, obstetricians, SCBU). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/preparing-for-the-birth/where-to-give-birth-the-options/
- **Birth partner(s)** and who attends if forceps/ventouse/caesarean (usually one partner allowed in theatre).
- **Pain relief preferences**: self-help/breathing/massage; **water** (37.5°C or less); **TENS** (early labour; hire one and practise; not in water); **gas and air** (works in 15 to 20 s); **pethidine/diamorphine** (20 min to work, lasts 2 to 4 hrs, can affect baby's breathing/first feed); **epidural** (labour ward only; headache about 1 in 100; may lengthen second stage); **remifentanil PCA**. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/what-happens/pain-relief-in-labour/
- **Positions/mobility**, monitoring preferences, induction/augmentation views.
- **Third stage**: oxytocin injection (managed) vs physiological; **delayed cord clamping: NICE NG235 1.10.14, not before 1 minute** unless concerns (Tommy's: 1 to 5 min; also possible at caesarean) https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/QS105/chapter/quality-statement-6-delayed-cord-clamping
- **Skin-to-skin immediately**, ideally before cord cut; **vitamin K** injection (single) vs oral (needs further doses) https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/after-the-birth/what-happens-straight-after/
- **Feeding plan** (breast/expressed/formula/combination), cutting the cord (dad?), photos/video, music, religious/cultural needs, special circumstances (previous trauma, disability, interpreter).
- **Partner's role as advocate**: NHS's 10 birth-partner tips: know the plan, read up, know the route and parking, charged phone and packed bag, encourage, help focus/breathe, help move positions, keep her fed/hydrated, **speak up to staff on her behalf**, look after yourself. You cannot consent for her; your job is to make sure she's asked and heard (Birthrights). Sources: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/what-happens/tips-for-your-birth-partner/ ; https://birthrights.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Birth-partners-factsheet-March-2025.docx.pdf ; https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/dads-and-partners/how-support-decision-making-and-speak-your-partner-and
- Gotcha: a 3-page birth plan nobody reads. Make a one-page version with the 5 things that matter most; bring 2 copies.

### B6. The hospital bag (pack by 36 weeks; NCT says have it ready by 37; some trusts say start at 32)

**Mum (labour bag)**: maternity notes/app login + birth plan copies; loose top/T-shirt nightie; dressing gown, slippers/flip-flops; TENS; water bottle with straw; lip balm; hair ties; fan/water spray; snacks; music/headphones; pillow from home; toiletries; glasses/contacts.
**Mum (postnatal)**: plenty of maternity pads ("you'll go through quite a few", Leeds), big dark knickers (5+), nursing bras and pads, nightwear that opens at front, going-home outfit (maternity size), towel, toiletries, phone charger (long cable; wall chargers may need PAT testing in some units), medication.
**Baby**: 3 to 5 vests and sleepsuits (newborn + 0 to 3), hat, scratch mitts, cardigan, blanket, newborn nappies (20), cotton wool/water wipes, muslins, going-home outfit, **car seat fitted in car**, formula starter pack if bottle-feeding (ready-made cartons + sterile teats).
**Dad's bag** (Which?/NHS trusts): snacks and drinks (hospital food is for her, not you), **change/card for parking and vending machines**, phone + battery pack + cable, toothbrush and deodorant, change of clothes, **swim shorts/T-shirt if planning a birth pool**, pillow and light blanket for the chair, book/downloaded entertainment (patchy wifi), camera/space on phone, printed phone list, copy of the birth plan, any medication, cash for the taxi if you've had a drink/no sleep. Leeds allows one birth partner to stay overnight in the bedside chair.
Sources: https://leedsth.nhs.uk/?p=4005 ; https://www.which.co.uk/reviews/giving-birth/article/what-to-pack-in-your-hospital-bag ; https://thh.nhs.uk/packing-your-hospital-bag ; https://www.nnuh.nhs.uk/departments/maternity-department/pregnancy/what-to-pack-in-your-hospital-bag/ ; https://www.leedsth.nhs.uk/services/maternity/after-birth/postnatal/
- Gotcha: packing the car seat in the boot *in its box*. Fit it. And **keep the car above a quarter tank**.

### B7. Car seat

- **Law**: child seat until **12 years or 135 cm**; approved seats carry an E-mark with **R129 (i-Size, height-based)** or **R44** (weight groups 0, 0+, 1, 2, 3); **i-Size seats must be rear-facing until over 15 months**; deactivate the front airbag if a rear-facing seat goes in the front; no side-facing seats; driver liable, fine up to **£500**. Sources: https://www.gov.uk/child-car-seats-the-rules ; https://www.which.co.uk/reviews/child-car-seats/article/child-car-seat-laws-uk-and-abroad/child-car-seat-laws-in-the-uk-ay4Sj1h8HGhU
- New **R44 seats can no longer be sold** since Sept 2024 (existing ones remain legal). Source: https://www.gumtree.com/info/cars/advice/safety-and-security/child-car-seat-safety-guide/
- **Taxi/minicab exemption**: under-3s may travel without a seat in a licensed taxi/PHV **only on the rear seat**; 3+ can use an adult belt. Unexpected short necessary journeys also exempt for 3+. Source: https://www.gov.uk/child-car-seats-the-rules/when-a-child-can-travel-without-a-car-seat
- **Choosing/fitting**: ISOFIX base vs belted; check the seat is approved for your car model (manufacturer fit-lists); try before you buy; practise fitting and removing; **get it checked** (retailer/council fitting events). Second-hand: only from someone you trust with full history, instructions and no crash/drop (RoSPA "one-hit" products). Sources: https://childcarseats.org.uk/choosing-using/second-hand-child-seats/ ; https://rospa.com/Home-Safety/Advice/Product/Second-hand-Goods
- **Sleep in car seats**: Lullaby Trust: for transport only; take baby out on arrival; breaks on long journeys; stop if baby slumps; remove hats/bulky coats in the car. Widely quoted "2-hour rule" is industry/parenting guidance rather than Lullaby Trust; Bristol research suggests under-4-week-olds show breathing/oxygen changes in as little as 30 minutes upright. Sources: https://www.lullabytrust.org.uk/safer-sleep-advice/car-seats/ ; https://www.motherandbaby.com/baby/safety/how-long-can-baby-be-in-car-seat
- Safety beyond the law: Which?/RoSPA recommend **extended rear-facing to about 4 years**.
- Gotcha: bulky snowsuits under the harness; harness at shoulder height; twisted straps; the newborn insert left in too long/removed too early.

### B8. Home prep

- **Sleep space (Lullaby Trust)**: baby **on their back**, in a **clear, flat, firm, waterproof mattress** in a cot/Moses basket/bedside crib, **in your room day and night for 6 months**; **16 to 20°C** with a room thermometer; no pillows, duvets, bumpers, soft toys, pods/nests, wedges or sleep positioners; lightweight bedding or a fitted sleeping bag; **smoke-free**; **never sleep on a sofa/armchair with baby**; about half of SIDS deaths occur while co-sleeping, mostly in high-risk situations (smoker, alcohol/drugs/sedating meds, premature under 37 weeks or under 2.5 kg). Dummy at sleep may reduce risk. Sources: https://www.lullabytrust.org.uk/safer-sleep-advice/ ; https://www.lullabytrust.org.uk/safer-sleep-advice/co-sleeping/
- **Pram/travel system**: must be **lie-flat from birth** (carrycot) until about 6 months; infant car seat clicks onto frame (handy, but not for long sleeps); check boot fit, weight, one-hand fold, car-seat compatibility/adaptors, rain cover. Source: https://www.silvercrossbaby.com/blogs/the-edit/travel-systems-explained ; https://www.johnlewis.com/content/baby-children/baby-checklist
- **Feeding kit**: if breastfeeding: nursing pillow, breast pads, nipple cream, a hand or electric pump (maybe hire), bottles for expressed milk; if formula: 6 bottles/teats, **steriliser (steam, cold-water or boiling 10 min or more; sterilise until 12 months)**, bottle brush, formula; prep machines may not reach 70°C. Sources: https://www.cambspborochildrenshealth.nhs.uk/feeding-and-eating/bottle-feeding/making-up-a-feed/ ; https://www.cambspborochildrenshealth.nhs.uk/feeding-and-eating/bottle-feeding/bottle-hygiene-and-equipment-sterilisation/
- **Nappies**: newborns need about 10 to 12 changes/day, about 4 poos/day in week 1, so about 70 to 80 nappies/week; size 1 (2 to 5 kg) but don't over-stock one size. Changing mat, cotton wool/water wipes, barrier cream, nappy bags/bin. Sources: https://sefton.gov.uk/childrens-services/schools-and-learning/early-years/families/your-childs-development/newborn ; https://lansinoh.co.uk/blogs/pregnancy/newborn-essentials-list
- **Clothing**: about 6 to 8 vests, 6 to 8 sleepsuits in each of **newborn/first size and 0 to 3 months** (newborn fits about 3 to 4 weeks for an average 3.4 kg baby), 2 to 3 cardigans, hats, scratch mitts, socks, pramsuit (season), 6+ muslins. Wash in non-bio before birth. Sources: https://www.johnlewis.com/content/baby-children/baby-checklist ; https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/pregnancy/3383414-Babys-first-clothes-sizes
- **Other**: baby bath/support, 2 soft towels, digital thermometer, nail scissors, Moses basket stand, blackout blind, night light, white-noise, changing bag, red book folder. **Baby-proofing (stair gates, socket covers, cupboard locks) is not needed until about 6 months.** Don't spend yet.
- **Batch cooking**: 2 to 3 weeks of freezer meals/one-handed snacks; label and date. **Deep clean** and declutter at 32 to 34 weeks (not 39). Set up online grocery delivery slots.
- **Pets** (Dogs Trust via NHS/Wirral): vaccinations/flea/worm up to date; introduce baby items (pram, car seat, blankets, wipes) to sniff; **stair/baby gates up early** so dog doesn't associate them with baby; play baby-cry recordings; train sit/stay/leave; bring home a hospital blanket with baby's scent before baby. Source: https://www.wchc.nhs.uk/children-young-people/parents-carers/new-parents/how-to-prepare-your-dog-for-a-new-baby/
- Gotcha: buying a cot bumper or a "nest" because it looks cosy. Lullaby Trust says no.

### B9. Logistics for the day

- **Numbers to save (Leeds)**: Maternity Assessment Centre **LGI 0113 392 6731**; **St James's 0113 206 5781** (24 hrs; phone before coming in); community midwife team; 111; GP; taxi firms; grandparents/childcare backup; dog walker/pet sitter; work. Source: https://www.leedsth.nhs.uk/services/maternity/pregnancy/mac/
- **When to call**: regular contractions **every 5 minutes or more often**, **waters break** (call immediately even at night), bleeding, reduced movements, worried. Latent phase can last hours or days; expect to be told to stay home. **999** if strong urge to push, baby's head or cord visible, heavy bleeding, seizure, collapse. **111** if you can't reach the unit. Sources: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/signs-of-labour/signs-that-labour-has-begun/ ; https://www.royalberkshire.nhs.uk/services-and-departments/maternity/giving-birth/signs-of-labour
- **Route**: drive it at the time of day you're likely to go (rush hour vs 3am); know the **alternative route** and the maternity entrance (LGI Clarendon Wing vs St James's Gledhow Wing; verify entrance at your tour); know **where to park and how to pay** (card/app/change); fuel/charge above a quarter.
- **If she's alone**: she calls MAC first, then you; if you're over 45 min away, arrange a nearby backup (neighbour/parent) who can drive; she knows the bag location and has a taxi app with payment set up; **tell work at 37 weeks that you may leave mid-shift** and agree how (phone on loud/exception to no-phones rules).
- **Other children/pets**: two named backups each, with keys, car seats and overnight bags staged.
- Timing contractions: use an app; note duration, frequency, and when they started. The midwife will ask.
- Gotcha: both phones dead at the crucial moment. Battery pack lives in the bag.

### B10. Relationship and mental health

- **Pregnancy symptoms**: hormones intensify emotions and mood swings (Tommy's); nausea 6 to 12+ weeks (some all pregnancy); tiredness; hot/short of breath later; practical help = cooking, chores, snacks/fluids, sleep protection, going to appointments. Sources: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/dads-and-partners/your-partners-emotions-during-pregnancy ; https://bewellwigan.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/What-can-I-do-A-guide-for-partners-during-pregnancy.pdf
- **Sex in pregnancy**: safe in an uncomplicated pregnancy; orgasm doesn't trigger miscarriage/early labour; late-pregnancy sex may cause harmless Braxton Hicks; **avoid if** heavy bleeding/low placenta, waters have broken, cervical weakness, history of preterm labour or multiples in late pregnancy; use condoms if any STI risk. Sources: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/sex/ ; https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/sex-pregnancy
- **Dad's perinatal mental health**: roughly **1 in 10 men experience mental health difficulties in the first 6 months** (Dad Matters/St Helens); studies put paternal depression at 7 to 30% (Health Foundation); NCT: fathers experience perinatal depression at a similar rate to mothers (about 1 in 7). Signs in men often look like **irritability/anger, withdrawal, overworking, drinking, physical symptoms**. 70% of dads say support for them is poor or non-existent (Tommy's 2020). Sources: https://news.sthelens.gov.uk/article/11803/New-local-support-programme-for-new-and-expectant-dads ; https://www.health.org.uk/improvement-projects/reaching-out-to-fathers-enhancing-engagement-in-the-perinatal-period ; https://www.nct.org.uk/life-parent/emotions/postnatal-depression-dads-10-things-you-should-know ; https://www.tommys.org/about-us/news-views/tommys-answers-call-support-dads-and-partners
- **Where to get help**: GP; **NHS Talking Therapies self-referral**; **PANDAS** helpline 0808 1961 776 (11am to 10pm daily) + monthly dads' call (last Monday, 8 to 9pm); **Dad Matters Leeds** 0113 244 2419; **Mind** 0300 123 3393; **CALM** 0800 58 58 58; **Samaritans** 116 123; **Tommy's Midwives** 0800 0147 800 (Mon to Fri 9 to 5, partners welcome); **Shout** text BABYBUDDY to 85258; Andy's Man Club (free weekly men's groups); Leeds Dads. Sources: https://pandasfoundation.org.uk/how-we-can-support-you/support-for-dads/ ; https://www.nnuh.nhs.uk/publication/download/paternal-how-are-you-dad-v1 ; https://www.mindwell-leeds.org.uk/services-directory/dad-matters/
- **Mum's mental health**: baby blues (tearful, anxious, irritable) common and resolves within about 2 weeks; **PND** if it continues/worsens, any time to a year post-birth; watch for hopelessness, not bonding, not sleeping when she can, thoughts of harm, and go to GP/HV urgently; **postpartum psychosis** (confusion, hallucinations, mania; rare, emergency). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/post-natal-depression/overview/
- **Visitors and boundaries**: agree **before birth** who visits in the first 2 weeks, how long, that visitors bring food and don't come ill, no kissing baby's face, wash hands, no unsolicited advice; Debrett's and midwives note constant visitors damage breastfeeding establishment and recovery. Put you in charge of the door. It's a classic dad job. Sources: https://debretts.com/the-etiquette-of-visiting-new-parents/ ; https://thegreenparent.co.uk/new-mothers-need-radical-rest/
- **Grandparents**: brief them on safe-sleep rules (they'll have slept babies on fronts), car seat law, formula prep changes, and the visitors plan.
- Gotcha: dads who "don't want to make it about me" and never say they're struggling. Build a fortnightly self check-in into the app.

### B11. Practical skills to learn before birth

- **Nappy changing**: 10 to 12/day at first; wipe front-to-back; cotton wool and water for first weeks; air time for nappy rash; barrier cream thinly; never leave baby on a raised surface. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn
- **Bathing / topping and tailing**: no need to bathe daily (2 to 3 times/week is fine); top and tail (face, neck, hands, bottom) with warm water; **plain water only for first month**; test water with elbow (about 37°C); never leave baby alone in water, even for a second; support head/neck. Source: https://www.healthiertogether.nhs.uk/new-parent-and-baby/washing-and-bathing-your-baby ; https://lnwh.nhs.uk/washing-and-bathing-your-baby
- **Umbilical cord**: keep clean and dry, water only, fold nappy below it; falls off at about 5 to 12 days; redness, smell or discharge means midwife/GP. Source: https://www.uhsussex.nhs.uk/?p=8871
- **Swaddling**: thin, breathable fabric (muslin), not tight around the hips (hip dysplasia risk), **always on back**, never over the head, don't add blankets, check neck for overheating; **stop at first signs of rolling** (can be about 2 months). Sources: https://www.healthychildren.org/english/ages-stages/baby/diapers-clothing/pages/swaddling-is-it-safe.aspx ; https://theburydirectory.co.uk/storage/2937/safe_sleep_products_checklist.pdf
- **Slings, TICKS**: Tight, In view at all times, Close enough to kiss, Keep chin off chest, Supported back. Source: https://theburydirectory.co.uk/storage/2937/safe_sleep_products_checklist.pdf
- **Winding/burping**: over shoulder, sitting upright on lap supporting chin, or face-down across lap; gentle pat/rub; muslin ready. Colic (excessive crying in waves, under 5 months) is common and passes. **Never shake a baby.** Soothing: suckle, sling/sway/sing, white noise, walk/pram, warm bath, stroke back. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/soothing-a-crying-baby/
- **Safe sleep rules** (recite them): back, clear flat firm, own space in your room 6 months, 16 to 20°C, smoke-free, feet-to-foot, no sofa. Source: https://www.lullabytrust.org.uk/safer-sleep-advice/
- **Feeding cues**: early: stirring, mouth opening, head turning/rooting; mid: stretching, hand-to-mouth, increasing movement; late: **crying** (calm first, then feed). Newborns feed 8 to 12+ times in 24 hrs; cluster feeding in evenings is normal. Source: https://www.justonenorfolk.nhs.uk/healthy-lifestyles/infant-feeding/breastfeeding/feeding-cues/
- **Supporting breastfeeding as a partner**: bring water/snacks every feed, position pillows, do skin-to-skin yourself, take all nappies/winding/bathing, protect her sleep (bring baby to her, settle after), learn latch signs so you can reassure or call for help, guard against unhelpful visitor comments; **National Breastfeeding Helpline 0300 100 0212**; **NCT Infant Feeding Line 0300 330 0700**; Fatherhood Institute evidence: breastfeeding continues longer when partners learn about it antenatally. Sources: https://www.suffolk.gov.uk/asset-library/nhs-startforlife-off-to-the-best-start-leaflet-20221.pdf ; https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/arts-and-culture/dads-word-make-happy-families-1990431
- **Formula safety**: fresh tap water boiled, left 30 min or less so still **70°C or more**; add powder to water (correct scoops), shake, **cool under running tap**, test on wrist; **one feed at a time**, discard leftovers; never microwave; bottled water and prep machines may not be safe/hot enough; sterilise all equipment until 12 months. Source: https://www.cambspborochildrenshealth.nhs.uk/feeding-and-eating/bottle-feeding/making-up-a-feed/ ; https://ulh.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Bottle-feeding-guidance-English-for-reference.pdf
- **Jaundice**: common; most visible about day 3; usually gone in about 2 weeks; signs: yellow skin/eyes/under tongue; **urgent (GP/midwife/111)** if not improving, dark urine or pale chalky poo; **999/A&E** if jaundiced **under 24 hours old**, very sleepy, not feeding, floppy, few wet nappies; treatment is phototherapy (about 48 hrs); frequent feeding (8 to 12 times/day) helps. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/jaundice-newborn/
- **Red flags in baby** (NHS): fit, blue/grey/pale/mottled skin, floppy or hard to wake, breathing difficulty, rash that doesn't fade under a glass, temperature 38°C or more or under 36°C, **no wet nappy in 8 hours**, cry that doesn't sound like theirs. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/soothing-a-crying-baby/
- Gotcha: dads learn nappies but not formula temperatures; the 70°C rule is the one most often broken.

### B12. Post-birth immediate (first 2 weeks)

- **In hospital**: skin-to-skin; delayed cord clamping; vitamin K; oxytocin for placenta; baby examined, weighed, banded; stitches if needed; move to postnatal ward; discharge may be within 6 hours if both well, often next day. Birth partner can stay overnight at Leeds (chair). Sources: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/after-the-birth/what-happens-straight-after/ ; https://www.chelwest.nhs.uk/services/maternity/our-services/giving-birth/visiting ; https://www.leedsth.nhs.uk/services/maternity/after-birth/postnatal/
- **NIPE** within **72 hours** (eyes, heart, hips, testes), repeated at **6 to 8 weeks**. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/newborn-screening/physical-examination/
- **Hearing screen** (AOAE) before discharge or by 4 to 5 weeks (max 3 months). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/newbornhearing
- **Midwife visits**: first within 36 hours of transfer home (NICE NG194); about day 3 (weight, jaundice, feeding); **day 5 heel-prick blood spot** (sickle cell, CF, congenital hypothyroidism, 7 metabolic conditions including PKU and MCADD; SMA in some areas; results by 6 weeks); day 10 to 14 discharge to **health visitor**, whose new-birth visit is 7 to 14 days (weigh, head-to-toe, feeding, safe sleep, immunisations, family wellbeing). Sources: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng194/resources/postnatal-care-pdf-66142082148037 ; https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/newborn-screening/blood-spot-test/ ; https://nelft.nhs.uk/new-birth-visits
- **Admin**: register birth (42 days; both attend), Child Benefit (48 hrs after registering), **register baby with GP** (child form; red book/birth certificate helpful; register same practice as a parent), update life insurance/will, employer notification that paternity leave has started (if "day of birth" trigger), nursery waiting lists if needed. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/gps/how-to-register-with-a-gp-surgery/
- **Mother's red flags, call MAC/999**: bleeding soaking more than 1 pad/hour or large clots, feeling faint/racing heart (haemorrhage); **severe headache with vision changes, vomiting, swelling, upper abdominal pain** (pre-eclampsia can first appear *after* birth); **sepsis**: fever/shivering, confusion/slurred speech, severe breathlessness, not passing urine 12 to 18 hrs, mottled/discoloured skin, "feel like I might die"; smelly discharge; calf pain/swelling or chest pain/breathlessness (clots). Lochia normally lasts 2 to 6 weeks. Sources: https://www.nnuh.nhs.uk/publication/download/information-for-mothers-following-the-birth-of-a-baby-signs-and-symptoms-of-serious-illness-v6 ; https://healthforunder5s.co.uk/sussex/?p=1904
- **Fourth trimester**: the 12 weeks after birth; baby adjusting to life outside; sleeps in short bursts with no day/night rhythm; wants holding; mum recovering physically and hormonally. Your job: nights/early mornings, meals, the door, the admin, the dog, and noticing mood. Source: https://www.birthingrampian.scot.nhs.uk/the-4th-trimester/
- **Mum's 6 to 8 week GP check**: offered to all; covers mental health screening, bleeding, wound/perineum healing, bladder/bowel, feeding, contraception. Source: https://www.england.nhs.uk/2023/12/nhs-boosts-health-support-for-new-mums/
- Gotcha: assuming the hospital registers the birth. In Leeds you must book Merrion House yourself.

## Key gotchas summary (for the app's "don't miss" list)
1. Paternity-leave notice is due about week 25 (15th week before EWC), not when baby arrives.
2. Day-one *leave* right is not a pay right. SPP still needs 26 weeks' service and £129/week.
3. Unmarried? Both attend birth registration or dad isn't on the certificate and has no parental responsibility.
4. Claim Child Benefit even if you'll opt out of payments (NI credits; backdating max 3 months).
5. Car seat: R129 rear-facing to 15+ months; airbag off in front; never second-hand of unknown history.
6. Safe sleep: clear, flat, firm, back, your room 6 months, 16 to 20°C, never the sofa.
7. Formula: 70°C or more water, one feed at a time.
8. Reduced movements or waters breaking: call MAC immediately, even at 3am.
9. Jaundice under 24 hours old, or heavy bleeding/severe headache in mum post-birth = emergency.
10. Dads get PND too (about 1 in 10); PANDAS/Dad Matters Leeds/GP.
