# When things go differently: loss, neonatal care and support

Research for review action 10: what the app says and offers if a pregnancy ends in loss, if the baby needs neonatal care, or if she needs extra care after the birth. Pages read on 1 November 2026. Every number and rule below comes from the page cited next to it. This is not clinical advice. The wording in the app built from it needs a bereavement midwife's review before release (content review log L1 to L6).

## Definitions

- **Stillbirth and miscarriage.** NHS: a stillbirth is when a baby is born dead after 24 completed weeks of pregnancy; if the baby dies before 24 completed weeks, it's known as a miscarriage or late foetal loss. The page suggests a specialist midwife can talk through next steps, and mentions baby-loss support groups without naming them. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stillbirth/
- Research 04, verification note 12, already records that "viable" is a hard word for families who have lost a baby, and that the NHS week 7 page calls miscarriage loss "in the first 23 weeks".

## Support lines

- **Sands (stillbirth and neonatal death).** Helpline 0808 164 3332, 10am to 3pm Monday to Friday, and 6pm to 9pm on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday evenings; closed on bank holidays unless the website says otherwise. Email helpline@sands.org.uk. For bereaved parents, grandparents, other family, friends and colleagues, and "anyone who has been affected by pregnancy or baby loss". It doesn't mention fathers by name. Source: https://www.sands.org.uk/support-you/how-we-offer-support/helpline
- **Miscarriage UK.** The working name of The Miscarriage Association (it says it was formerly known as the Miscarriage Association). Helpline 0303 003 6464, 9am to 8pm Monday, Wednesday and Friday, 9am to 4pm Tuesday and Thursday. Its home page has a "supporting a loved one" link but nothing aimed at partners by name. Source: https://www.miscarriageuk.org/
- **Tommy's.** Midwives' line 0800 0147 800 (hours not given on the baby loss page). Source: https://www.tommys.org/baby-loss-support . A page for dads, partners and non-birthing parents says they have experienced loss too, and covers their own feelings, supporting a partner, going back to work and supporting siblings; it offers a baby loss support group, not a separate helpline. Source: https://www.tommys.org/baby-loss-support/dads-and-partners
- **Bliss (premature and sick babies).** Free services for families of babies in neonatal care, including a video call support service; emotional and practical support by email on weekdays. Over 90,000 babies are born premature or sick and admitted to neonatal care in the UK every year. Sources: https://www.bliss.org.uk/parents/support and https://support.bliss.org.uk
- **Samaritans.** 116 123, free from any phone, 24 hours a day. Their email service has closed in the UK. Source: https://www.samaritans.org/how-we-can-help/contact-samaritan/

## Leave and registration (UK)

- **Paternity leave after a stillbirth.** GOV.UK: you can still get Paternity Pay and Leave if the baby is stillborn from 24 weeks of pregnancy. If the baby dies after birth, you can take any leave you booked before, within 8 weeks of the death, and may also be eligible for Parental Bereavement Pay and Leave. Source: https://www.gov.uk/paternity-pay-leave/eligibility
- **Parental Bereavement Leave.** GOV.UK: you and your partner may be able to take time off if your child dies before they turn 18, or if you have a stillbirth after 24 weeks of pregnancy. Source: https://www.gov.uk/parental-bereavement-pay-leave . The employer guide: 2 weeks' leave for each child (together, as two separate weeks, or one week only), finishing within 56 weeks of the death or stillbirth. Source: https://www.gov.uk/employers-parental-bereavement-pay-leave
- **Registering a stillbirth.** GOV.UK: a stillbirth should be registered within 42 days (a register office can sometimes accept it later); Scotland has 21 days and Northern Ireland up to a year. If the parents are married or in a civil partnership, either can register. The baby can be named in the register. The register office says which documents are needed. Source: https://www.gov.uk/register-stillbirth
- **Neonatal Care Pay and Leave.** GOV.UK: if a newborn is premature or sick and needs neonatal care that starts in the first 28 days after birth, both parents may be able to get paid time off: one week for every 7 full and continuous days in neonatal care, up to 12 weeks, in addition to other parental pay and leave. Eligibility details are on a separate page. Source: https://www.gov.uk/neonatal-care-pay-leave

## What the app does with this

- Plain words, no humour and no space theme on any of these screens, whatever the humour setting.
- No claims about how anyone is doing in a message he sends; he writes that part, or leaves it out.
- Support lines are shown, not pushed: no reminders or notifications about them.
- Not covered yet, and needing more research: support for her own physical recovery after a difficult birth (she needs extra care), the Birth Trauma Association, twins, and US equivalents. Until then the US version shows only "ask the hospital team".
