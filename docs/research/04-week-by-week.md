# Week-by-Week Briefing: Baby, Bump and Partner (UK / NHS-led)

Author: Liam Daly
Research date: 26 October 2026.

This report is the content source for the weekly "briefing" in the Mission Control app: what the baby is doing, what the pregnant partner may be going through, what the dad can do about it, and which warning signs matter at that stage. It covers weeks 4 to 41 one week at a time and fills the gap left by report 01 (dad's to-dos, appointments, money and leave, labour). The backbone is the NHS week-by-week guide (now branded "Best Start in Life" on nhs.uk), checked against other nhs.uk condition pages, the NHS antenatal appointments schedule, NICE NG201, RCOG patient information and Tommy's week-by-week pages. Baby sizes are quoted exactly as the source states them. Where the NHS gives no number (it gives no weights for weeks 4 to 40), this report says so and only adds a figure if Tommy's or NHS inform (NHS Scotland) states one. Size comparisons are editorial suggestions written for the app, not clinical facts. Nothing here is a diagnosis: the "Watch for" lines repeat the sources' own "call your midwife / maternity unit / 111 / 999" triggers, and the app should present them the same way.

---

## How to read this report

- **Baby / Her / How he can help / Watch for / Size comparison** for every week. Source URLs sit inline next to the facts they support.
- **Measurement type.** Until week 19 the NHS measures "from head to bottom" (crown-to-rump). From **week 20** the NHS says: "Measurements are now taken from head to heel. In earlier weeks, babies are measured from the head to the bottom because the legs are curled up and hard to see." Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-20/ . So the jump from 15.3cm (week 19) to 25.6cm (week 20) is a change of method, not a growth spurt. The app should say this out loud at week 20.
- **Weights.** The NHS week pages give no weight for any week from 4 to 40. Week 41 gives "around 3 to 4kg". The few weights in this report come from Tommy's (weeks 17 and 34) and NHS inform (weeks 21 to 22, 35, 36, and a daily gain for 37 to 40). Every other week is marked "not stated".
- **Size comparisons** are marked "editorial suggestion". The dimensions given for each object are typical, approximate sizes, not measurements of a particular product. None are fruit or vegetables (the NHS pages themselves use fruit and veg, e.g. "about the size of a plum").
- **Appointments** are cross-checked against the NHS schedule in report 01 (Part B3) and the current NHS antenatal care page: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/

## Standing alerts (apply across many weeks; the weekly "Watch for" lines point back here)

1. **Bleeding (any stage).** NHS: get urgent help (maternity unit; early pregnancy unit if under 20 weeks; NHS 111 if neither) for light bleeding or spotting. **Call 999** if bleeding with severe tummy pain, pain in the shoulder, feeling sick, faint or dizzy, or heavy bleeding that soaks a pad soon after putting it on. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vaginal-bleeding/ . NICE: unexplained vaginal bleeding after 13 weeks should be referred to hospital for review. Source: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng201/chapter/Recommendations
2. **Ectopic pregnancy (about weeks 4 to 12).** NHS: symptoms usually develop between the 4th and 12th weeks: tummy pain low down on one side, unusual vaginal bleeding, shoulder tip pain, discomfort when peeing or pooing. Contact GP or 111. **Call 999** for sharp, sudden, intense tummy pain with feeling very dizzy or fainting, or feeling sick. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ectopic-pregnancy/symptoms/
3. **Severe sickness (first trimester onward).** NHS: call the midwife, GP or 111 if she is vomiting and has very dark pee or no pee in more than 8 hours, cannot keep food or fluids down for 24 hours, feels very weak, dizzy or faint when standing, has tummy pain, a high temperature, vomits blood or has lost weight. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vomiting-and-morning-sickness/ . Severe sickness (hyperemesis gravidarum) affects around 1 to 3 in 100 and often needs hospital treatment. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/complications/severe-vomiting/
4. **Baby's movements (once established; NHS says first felt between 16 and 24 weeks).** NHS: call the midwife or maternity unit **immediately** if the baby is moving less than usual, she cannot feel the baby moving any more, or the usual pattern changes. "Do not wait until the next day – call immediately, even if it's the middle of the night." There is no set number of kicks to count, and home dopplers should not be used. If she has not felt the baby move by 24 weeks, tell the midwife. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ . It is not true that babies move less towards the end of pregnancy; movements should increase until about 32 weeks and then stay roughly the same up to labour. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/pregnancy-symptom-checker/baby-fetal-movements . NICE: after 24+0 weeks, contact maternity services at any time of day or night with any concern about movements. Source: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng201/chapter/Recommendations . RCOG: "Never go to sleep ignoring a reduction or change in your baby's movements." Source: https://www.rcog.org.uk/for-the-public/browse-our-patient-information/your-babys-movements-in-pregnancy/
5. **Pre-eclampsia (most likely from 20 weeks; can also appear after the birth).** NHS symptoms: severe headache that does not go with simple painkillers; vision problems such as blurring or flashing lights; pain below the ribs; sudden swelling of the face, hands or feet; feeling very unwell; heartburn that does not go with heartburn medicines; being sick. "Any of these symptoms can be serious and need to be checked immediately": call the maternity unit, or 111 if that is not possible. Blood pressure and pee are checked at every appointment because the early signs are usually not noticeable. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pre-eclampsia/ . The same list appears on the NHS swelling and headache pages: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/swollen-ankles-feet-and-fingers/ ; https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/headaches/ . RCOG: about 1 in 200 women develop severe pre-eclampsia. Source: https://www.rcog.org.uk/for-the-public/browse-our-patient-information/pre-eclampsia/
6. **Itching (ICP).** NHS: get urgent help if the skin is very itchy (for example keeping her awake), the palms or soles are itchy, or itching is worse at night. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/complications/itching-and-intrahepatic-cholestasis/ . RCOG: ICP itching usually begins after 28 weeks, with no rash; it affects about 7 in 1000 women in the UK. Source: https://www.rcog.org.uk/for-the-public/browse-our-patient-information/intrahepatic-cholestasis-of-pregnancy/
7. **Premature labour (before 37 weeks).** NHS: call the midwife or maternity unit for regular contractions or tightenings, period-type pains, a gush or trickle of fluid from the vagina, or backache that is not usual for her. About 8 in 100 babies are born prematurely. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/premature-labour-and-birth/
8. **Labour and waters (from about 37 weeks, or earlier as above).** NHS: call the midwife or maternity unit if contractions come every 5 minutes or more often. Call **urgently** if waters break, there is vaginal bleeding, the baby is moving less, she is under 37 weeks and thinks she is in labour, any contraction lasts longer than 2 minutes, or there are 6 or more contractions every 10 minutes. **Call 999** if the baby is coming now and she has a strong urge to push. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/signs-that-labour-has-begun/
9. **Stomach pain.** NHS: call the maternity unit immediately for stomach pain with bleeding or spotting, regular cramping or tightenings, lower back pain, unusual discharge or leaking fluid, pain or urgency when peeing, cloudy, pink, red or brown pee, or pain that is severe or does not ease after 30 to 60 minutes' rest. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/stomach-pain/
10. **Breathlessness and clots.** Tommy's: contact the doctor or midwife straight away if breathlessness starts suddenly or gets much worse, she is breathless lying flat, both legs, feet or ankles swell, or she is coughing or wheezing; call 999 for palpitations with severe breathlessness, chest pain, dizziness or fainting. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/32-weeks-pregnant . NHS: call 999 or go to A&E for possible DVT symptoms (pain and swelling) with shortness of breath or chest pain. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt/
11. **Mental health (any stage).** NHS: some people get depression or anxiety for the first time in pregnancy; speak to the maternity unit or GP if worried, and get urgent help in a crisis. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/mental-health-in-pregnancy-and-after-the-birth/mental-health/ . Tommy's (for partners): partners can also speak to the GP, midwife or health visitor if they are worried about her. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/dads-and-partners/your-partners-emotions-during-pregnancy

## FIRST TRIMESTER (weeks 4 to 12)

### Week 4
**Baby:** The embryo is about 2mm long and growing rapidly. It sits in an amniotic sac of cushioning fluid, attached to a tiny yolk sac that supplies its nourishment. Its outer layer will later develop into the placenta. Size: **about 2mm long** (measurement type not stated). Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-4/ . Tommy's agrees: "a little dot, measuring about 2mm". Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/4-weeks-pregnant

**Her:** She probably does not look pregnant (first-time mums may not show until at least week 12). Pregnancy is dated from the first day of her last period. She may have no symptoms yet; possible early signs include a missed period, a metallic taste, sore breasts, nausea at any time of day, tiredness, needing to pee more, light spotting as the egg implants, and period-like cramping. Advice: 400 micrograms of folic acid daily until at least week 12, and to consider 10 micrograms of vitamin D daily between October and early March (all year round for some people). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-4/ . **Due:** tell maternity services (self-referral or GP); the first midwife (booking) appointment is at 8 to 12 weeks and takes about 1 hour. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/

**How he can help:**
- Help her refer herself for NHS maternity care and put the 8-to-12-week booking window in the shared calendar. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/
- Make the folic acid and vitamin D a no-effort daily habit (by the kettle, phone reminder). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-4/
- If anyone in the house smokes, quit or keep smoke right away from her; Tommy's says it helps her and the baby if household smokers quit too, and if they cannot, they should not smoke near her or in or near the house. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/6-weeks-pregnant

**Watch for:** Bleeding at any stage should be checked (Standing alert 1); Tommy's: get any bleeding checked by a doctor, midwife or Early Pregnancy Assessment Unit. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/4-weeks-pregnant . Ectopic symptoms (Standing alert 2).

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** the head of a pin (about 2mm).

### Week 5
**Baby:** The nervous system is developing and the brain and spinal cord are taking shape. The tiny heart is forming and the NHS says it "will beat for the first time around now" (sources differ, see Verification notes). The face is starting to form, the baby has some of its own blood vessels, a string of which will become the umbilical cord, and the placenta is being created. Size: **around 2mm long** (measurement type not stated). Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-5/ . Tommy's: the neural tube (future brain and spinal cord) is forming now, which is what folic acid supports. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/5-weeks-pregnant

**Her:** Many women realise they are pregnant around week 5: a late period, feeling under the weather, extreme tiredness. Cravings are possible; unusual cravings (for example for dirt) should be raised with the midwife or doctor. Around 1 in 5 mums feel anxious or stressed during pregnancy. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-5/ . **Due:** nothing new; booking appointment still to arrange.

**How he can help:**
- If she is anxious, make space to talk and suggest she raises it with the midwife or doctor; NHS also suggests breathing exercises. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-5/
- Take over the jobs that make tiredness worse (shopping, cooking, cleaning). Tommy's for partners: help her eat well and get plenty of rest. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/dads-and-partners/getting-involved-in-the-pregnancy
- Start a shared note of questions for the booking appointment (editorial).

**Watch for:** Bleeding and ectopic symptoms (Standing alerts 1 and 2).

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** the thickness of a £1 coin (about 3mm).

### Week 6
**Baby:** The baby is growing arms, legs and ears, and the liver, brain and musculoskeletal system are developing. It looks a bit like a tadpole with a little tail; arm and leg "limb buds" are forming, there are tiny dents where the ears will be, and bulges for the heart and head. A heartbeat can sometimes be seen on a vaginal scan, but this is unlikely to be offered unless she has had IVF. Size: **around 6mm long** (measurement type not stated). Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-6/ . Tommy's: "At around week 6, their heart will beat for the first time." Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/6-weeks-pregnant

**Her:** Morning sickness can strike at any time but is often worse on waking. Also tiredness, mood swings, headaches, sore breasts and a heightened sense of smell. NHS tips: a snack by the bed, 6 small meals a day, rest, water, ginger, ice cubes, acupressure bands; talk to the midwife or doctor if she cannot keep food down. Many people feel better after 12 weeks. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-6/ . Around 8 in 10 pregnant women have nausea, vomiting or both, and for most it improves or stops by around weeks 12 to 20. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/complications/severe-vomiting/

**How he can help:**
- Put a snack and water by her side of the bed every night. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-6/
- Help work out her triggers (a food, a smell, a time of day) and take over cooking anything strong-smelling. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/dads-and-partners/getting-involved-in-the-pregnancy
- Keep ginger biscuits or ginger tea in the house. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-6/

**Watch for:** Severe sickness signs (Standing alert 3); bleeding and ectopic symptoms (alerts 1 and 2).

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** the end of a standard pencil (about 7mm across).

### Week 7
**Baby:** Around 10mm long from head to bottom. The brain is growing faster than the rest of the body, so the forehead is large; there are dimples where the nose and ears will be, and the eyelids are beginning to form. The limb buds are forming cartilage, and the arm buds are lengthening with flattened ends that will become hands. The embryo is generating around 100 new brain cells every minute. Size: **around 10mm, head to bottom** (crown-to-rump). Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-7/

**Her:** Her blood volume is rising and will increase by up to 50% over the pregnancy, which can make her thirstier (aim for 8 medium glasses of fluid a day). Her womb is about the size of a lemon. Sickness, tiredness and mood swings can make this a challenging week. She does not legally have to tell her employer until the 15th week before the due date. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-7/ . **Due:** booking appointment approaching (8 to 12 weeks). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/

**How he can help:**
- Keep a filled water bottle within her reach at home and in the car. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-7/
- Agree together who you tell and when; NHS: "entirely up to you". Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-7/
- Put a provisional due date in the calendar using the NHS due date calculator, knowing the dating scan will refine it. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-7/

**Watch for:** NHS: don't ignore strange symptoms such as feeling itchy all over; talk to the midwife or doctor. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-7/ . Bleeding, ectopic and sickness alerts (1 to 3).

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a AAA battery seen end-on (about 1cm across).

### Week 8
**Baby:** Around 16mm long and growing very quickly. The head has started to uncurl a bit; the arms are getting longer and are bigger than the legs, and the knees, ankles, thighs and toes have not developed yet. Around now the embryo becomes a "foetus". The placenta is sprouting little branches to attach itself to the wall of the womb. Size: **around 16mm long** (measurement type not stated). Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-8/ . Tommy's: "starting to look more like a little person". Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/8-weeks-pregnant-whats-happening

**Her:** Bloating, slightly swollen breasts, tiredness and sickness; peeing more often as the womb pushes on the bladder (NHS: drink lots in the day but less in the evening if it disturbs sleep). There may be times she feels anxious and stressed. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-8/ . **Due:** the booking appointment (8 to 12 weeks, about 1 hour): questions about her health, previous pregnancies, mental health, lifestyle, job, home situation and ethnic background; blood pressure, pee test, blood group; screening information; maternity notes issued. Partners can come if she would like. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ . NICE recommends offering the booking appointment by 10+0 weeks. Source: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng201/chapter/Recommendations

**How he can help:**
- Go to the booking appointment if she wants you there, and expect the midwife may see her alone for a few minutes first. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/dads-and-partners/pregnancy-scans-and-appointments
- Bring your own family medical history; the midwife asks about family history (see report 01, Part B3).
- If night-time loo trips are wrecking her sleep, help her front-load drinks earlier in the day. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-8/

**Watch for:** Bleeding, ectopic and sickness alerts (1 to 3).

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a standard board-game dice (about 16mm).

### Week 9
**Baby:** Around 22mm long from head to bottom. The face looks more recognisable, with eyes protected by eyelids, a little mouth and a tongue with tiny taste buds. Hands and feet are developing, with grooves where fingers and toes will be. The heart, brain, lungs, kidneys and gut are all developing and bones are starting to form. The genitals are taking shape, though the sex usually is not known until the anomaly scan. Size: **around 22mm, head to bottom**. Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-9/

**Her:** The pregnancy hormone hCG peaks around now, with higher oestrogen and progesterone too; some describe an "emotional rollercoaster". Extreme tiredness, sickness and mood swings are common, but most start to feel better week by week towards the second trimester. Eating little and often keeps blood sugar stable and can reduce sickness. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-9/

**How he can help:**
- Stock easy "little and often" snacks; NHS ideas include a boiled egg with wholemeal soldiers, hummus with carrot sticks, and pitta bread with grated cheese. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-9/
- Line up practical help from family and friends; NHS: "get lots of rest and accept any offers of help". Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-9/

**Watch for:** Tommy's: contact the GP, midwife or hospital straight away if sickness makes daily life very difficult. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/9-weeks-pregnant . Standing alerts 1 to 3.

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a £1 coin (about 23mm across).

### Week 10
**Baby:** Around 30mm long from head to bottom. The baby makes jerky movements that can be seen on a scan. The head is still big for the body but the face is more in proportion; the eyes are half closed but can react to light; the ears are forming, there is an upper lip and 2 nostrils, and the jaw contains tiny versions of the milk teeth. The NHS gives the heart rate as about 180bpm (another NHS page differs; see Verification notes). Size: **around 30mm, head to bottom**. Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-10/

**Her:** Her jeans may be getting tight; her womb is around the size of a large orange. Progesterone relaxes muscles, including in the gut, which can cause bloating, burping, wind and heartburn. Other symptoms include dizziness and greasier, spotty skin. NHS tips: 6 small meals a day, avoid eating late, eat slowly, a short stroll after meals. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-10/ . **Due:** booking appointment around now if not done. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-10/

**How he can help:**
- Move the evening meal earlier, keep portions smaller, and go for a short walk together afterwards. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-10/
- Make sure the midwife or GP has applied for her maternity exemption certificate (free prescriptions and NHS dental care; form FW8 via midwife, GP or health visitor). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-18/

**Watch for:** Stomach pain with any of the features in Standing alert 9. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/stomach-pain/ . Alerts 1 to 3.

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a £2 coin (about 28mm across).

### Week 11
**Baby:** Around 41mm long from head to bottom. The head is still "supersized" but the body is growing quickly. Fingers and toes are separating, and there are tiny fingernails and miniature ears. The baby is kicking around, though she probably will not feel anything for several weeks. Size: **around 41mm, head to bottom**. Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-11/

**Her:** As the bump starts to show, stretching muscles and ligaments can cause pains around her stomach. Up to 50% more blood than usual can make her feel hot, sweaty and dizzy. The placenta is taking over from the yolk sac, and the hormones involved can make her tired and emotional. Blood, urine and blood pressure tests are offered around now. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-11/ . **Due:** the dating scan, with optional screening for Down's, Edwards' and Patau's syndromes. The NHS schedule gives it as 11 to 14 weeks. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/

**How he can help:**
- Find out how the hospital books the dating scan, whether she needs a full bladder, and book the time off (it takes about 20 minutes). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/12-week-scan/ . This is a good use of one of your two unpaid antenatal appointment slots (report 01, Part B1).
- Talk through the screening choice together before the day: the combined test (blood test plus a neck-fluid measurement on the scan) is her choice to accept or decline. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/12-week-scan/

**Watch for:** NHS: if the bump pains hurt a lot, see the midwife or doctor as soon as possible. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-11/ . Alerts 1 to 3.

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a golf ball (about 43mm across).

### Week 12
**Baby:** The baby is 5.4cm long from head to bottom. The internal organs and muscles have grown, and the heartbeat can be picked up on an ultrasound scan. The skeleton is tissue that is now hardening into bone. The sex organs have formed, although most scans will not show the sex until later. Size: **5.4cm, head to bottom** (crown-to-rump). Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-12/ . Tommy's: "although tiny, is now fully formed", and the placenta is now fully formed too. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/12-weeks-pregnant

**Her:** There's a good chance the placenta is now feeding the baby, and once that happens her hormones calm down a bit. Her waist is thickening and her breasts are bigger; as the sickness eases she may feel hungrier. Most mums-to-be put on 10kg to 12.5kg, mostly after week 20; no extra calories are needed until the third trimester (then about 200 a day). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-12/ . **Due:** the dating scan, which the NHS scan page says is offered at around 10 to 14 weeks. It checks how many weeks pregnant she is and the due date, whether there is more than one baby, that the baby is growing in the right place, and the baby's development. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/12-week-scan/

- Be at the dating scan (about 20 minutes; she may need a full bladder). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/12-week-scan/

## SECOND TRIMESTER (weeks 13 to 27)

### Week 13
**Baby:** Around 7.4cm long (the NHS does not say how it is measured this week; weeks either side are head to bottom). The ovaries or testes are fully developed inside. The baby is moving around; movements start jerky and random and then look more deliberate. Some babies suck their thumb, which helps develop the sucking reflex they will need for feeding. Size: **around 7.4cm** (measurement type not stated). Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-13/ . NHS inform gives about 7cm head to bottom at weeks 13 to 14. Source: https://www.nhsinform.scot/ready-steady-baby/pregnancy/your-baby-s-development/how-your-baby-develops-week-to-week/

**Her:** She is a third of the way through and hopefully past the worst of the symptoms. A small bump may show; peeing more often should ease as the womb moves up away from the bladder. More blood in the pelvic area increases some women's sex drive, and sex is safe unless she has been told otherwise. Swollen, bleeding gums are common, and NHS dental care is free in pregnancy and for a year after. Other symptoms can include "round ligament" pains on the side of the belly, nosebleeds, constipation, heartburn, leg cramps, feeling hot, dizziness and swollen hands and feet. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-13/

**How he can help:**
- Take over the cat litter tray permanently (cat faeces can carry toxoplasmosis). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-13/
- Book her a dentist check-up (free with the maternity exemption certificate). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-13/
- Keep her away from anyone with chickenpox or rubella and tell the midwife straight away about any contact. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-13/

**Watch for:** Urine infections are listed among the symptoms; stomach pain with pain when peeing or discoloured pee needs a call (Standing alert 9). Unexplained bleeding after 13 weeks should be reviewed in hospital (alert 1). Source: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng201/chapter/Recommendations

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a cricket ball (about 7cm across).

### Week 14
**Baby:** Around 8.5cm long from head to bottom. The head is getting rounder and more in proportion. The baby is kicking but she probably will not feel it yet; the midwife might hear the heartbeat with a handheld monitor. The baby swallows small amounts of amniotic fluid, and the kidneys pass it back out as urine. Size: **around 8.5cm, head to bottom**. Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-14/ . Tommy's gives the same 8.5cm and warns that home dopplers and apps claiming to monitor the heartbeat are misleading and can be dangerous. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/14-weeks-pregnant

**Her:** Hopefully she is less tired and sick, and her appetite may come back (the baby does not need extra calories yet). She may notice yellow stains in her bra: probably colostrum, the first milk. Relationships can come under strain in pregnancy; NHS advises talking about feelings. Sleeping on her front is fine for now, but after 28 weeks it is safest to go to sleep on her side. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-14/

**How he can help:**
- Keep healthy snacks around now her appetite is back. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-14/
- Have the "how are we both doing?" conversation; NHS says to talk about feelings and speak to the midwife or doctor if either of you is not coping. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-14/
- Do not buy a home doppler. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/

**Watch for:** Ask the midwife about any breast changes that worry her. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-14/ . Alerts 1 and 9.

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a bank card, long edge (about 8.6cm).

### Week 15
**Baby:** Around 10.1cm long from head to bottom. The baby has grown a soft layer of hair (lanugo) all over, eyebrows and eyelashes are starting, and the eyes are sensitive to light. Around now the baby will start hearing: her voice, her heartbeat and her digestive system. Size: **around 10.1cm, head to bottom**. Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-15/ . Tommy's: hiccups may start around now (hiccups do not count as movements). Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/15-weeks-pregnant

**Her:** As the bump grows she will be less steady on her feet, so she should ask for a seat on public transport. Jabbing "round ligament" pains on the sides of the bump ease with rest and feet up. Her skin may feel itchy (unperfumed moisturiser, loose cotton clothes, a cool bath help). More vaginal discharge is common. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-15/

**How he can help:**
- Start talking to the bump; NHS says the baby will probably hear you. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-15/
- Pick up an unperfumed moisturiser if she is itchy. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-15/

**Watch for:** NHS: talk to the doctor or midwife if the itching gets worse, especially at night (Standing alert 6), or if discharge changes colour, smells bad, changes texture, or comes with pain peeing, itching or soreness. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-15/

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a computer mouse (roughly 10 to 12cm long).

### Week 16
**Baby:** Around 11.6cm long from head to bottom. The baby pulls faces, though any smile or frown is random. The developing nervous system lets the baby move arms and legs, and the hands can make fists. Size: **around 11.6cm, head to bottom**. Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-16/ . Tommy's: she may feel movements from 16 weeks if this is not her first baby; first-time mums may not notice until after 20 weeks. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/16-weeks-pregnant

**Her:** The 16-week appointment: the midwife discusses results of the booking blood tests (including HIV, hepatitis B and syphilis), checks blood pressure and tests urine for protein (a pre-eclampsia check), and she may hear the heartbeat. Constipation is common (fibre, exercise, water help). Carbon monoxide testing is now routine at appointments. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-16/ ; https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ . **Vaccines:** the whooping cough vaccine can be given from 16 weeks (usually around 20; best before 32). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/whooping-cough-vaccination/ . The flu vaccine can be given at any stage of pregnancy during the flu season (the current season's last date is 31 March 2027). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/vaccinations/flu-vaccine/

**How he can help:**
- Check the home carbon monoxide detector works (press the test button; replace if it does not beep). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-16/

- Cook high-fibre meals (wholemeal bread, fruit and veg, beans, lentils). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-16/

**Watch for:** Pre-eclampsia usually has no symptoms early, which is why every appointment matters (Standing alert 5). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pre-eclampsia/

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a 330ml drinks can (about 11.5cm tall).

### Week 17
**Baby:** Around 12cm long from head to bottom. The baby can move their eyes (still shut), react to loud noises, and open and close their mouth. Fingernails are growing, with unique fingerprints. Size: **around 12cm, head to bottom** (NHS). Weight: NHS not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-17/ . **Her:** She might feel the first movements now (bubbling, fluttering, rolling); many women first notice them between 18 and 24 weeks. **Watch for:** If she feels sad or worried, NHS says talk to the doctor or midwife (Standing alert 11). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-17/

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a CD (12cm across). By weight (Tommy's 150g): roughly a smartphone.

**How he can help:**

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a standard UK pillow, short side (about 48 to 50cm).

### Week 37
**Baby:** Around 48.6cm long from head to heel. The baby is trying out facial expressions such as frowning and smiling (random, not linked to mood) and is now "full term": probably big and mature enough to cope outside the womb. Around 95% of babies are now head down. Size: **around 48.6cm, head to heel**. Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-37/ . NHS inform (weeks 37 to 40): putting on about 14g a day. Source: https://www.nhsinform.scot/ready-steady-baby/pregnancy/your-baby-s-development/how-your-baby-develops-week-to-week/

**Her:** More vaginal discharge and Braxton Hicks; a possible sudden urge to spring clean ("nesting"; NHS says don't overdo it). As the baby drops, heartburn and constant peeing may ease. Twin pregnancies rarely go beyond 38 weeks. Signs labour may be starting: contractions or tightenings, a "show", back pain, an urge to go to the toilet, waters breaking. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-37/

**How he can help:**
- Learn the labour signs and the "when to call" rules together (Standing alert 8). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/signs-that-labour-has-begun/
- Read up on postnatal depression now; NHS suggests she lets her partner know about it. Signs include low mood, loss of interest, exhaustion, poor sleep, withdrawing, and frightening thoughts. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-37/
- Do the nesting jobs she wants done so she can rest. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-37/

**Watch for:** Tommy's: a high temperature could be a sign of a hidden infection; call the maternity unit straight away. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/37-weeks-pregnant . Labour alerts (alert 8).

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a 22-inch computer monitor (roughly 49 to 50cm wide).

### Week 38
**Baby:** Around 49.8cm long from head to heel. Most of the downy lanugo has gone (some babies are born with patches). The baby is storing sticky green meconium in the bowels, which normally comes out after birth as the first poo. Size: **around 49.8cm, head to heel**. Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-38/ . Tommy's: "nearly 50cm from head to heel"; the baby has all the space they need and should not be moving less. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/38-weeks-pregnant

**Her:** Frustration or boredom is a common new "symptom". Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-38/ . Tommy's: this can be made worse by well-meaning comments such as "not long now!". Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/38-weeks-pregnant . **Due:** 38-week appointment: bump measured, blood pressure checked, urine tested for protein (pre-eclampsia can happen in the second half of pregnancy or after the birth), and a chat about what happens beyond 41 weeks. A planned caesarean is usually booked at 39 weeks or later. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-38/ ; https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/

**How he can help:**
- Be the family's single point of contact so she is not fielding "any news?" messages. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/38-weeks-pregnant
- Go for walks together for fresh air, taking her notes just in case. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-38/
- Know the plan if she goes past 41 weeks (sweep, induction, monitoring). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-38/

**Watch for:** NHS: if the baby does a poo during labour it can be a sign of distress. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-38/ . Waters that are smelly or coloured, or losing blood: tell the midwife immediately. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/signs-that-labour-has-begun/ . Pre-eclampsia signs (alert 5).

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** an acoustic guitar body (roughly 50cm long).

### Week 39
**Baby:** Around 50.7cm long from head to heel. A tougher new skin layer is growing to protect the organs and help with temperature control, coated in vernix, which may be mostly gone by birth. Size: **around 50.7cm, head to heel**. Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-39/ . Tommy's: the immune system can now protect against a variety of infections, mainly thanks to her antibodies. Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/39-weeks-pregnant

**Her:** Pregnancy normally lasts around 40 weeks, and most women go into labour within a week either side of the due date. More discharge; a "show" (a slimy, yellow or bloody blob of mucus) can be one of the first signs, but labour could still be days away. Back pain and pressure at the bottom of the bump; nesting bursts. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-39/ . **Due:** a membrane sweep is usually offered after 39 weeks. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/inducing-labour/

**How he can help:**
- Stay on full alert for her waters breaking: it may be just a trickle. If you think they have, call the midwife or doctor for advice. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-39/
- Know the phone trigger: contractions lasting at least 60 seconds and coming every 5 minutes, or any time something seems wrong. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-39/
- Learn the NHS "9 signs you should not ignore" (below). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-39/

**Watch for:** NHS week 39 lists signs to treat as an emergency (call the midwife, doctor or NHS 111): bleeding from the vagina; brown or pink discharge; severe itching, particularly at night; a terrible headache that will not go away; vision problems; pain just below the ribs; extreme swelling of the feet, ankles, hands and face; persistent stomach pains; a high temperature above 37.5°C with no other flu or cold symptoms. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-39/

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** your own forearm, elbow to fingertip (roughly 45 to 50cm), plus a little.

### Week 40
**Baby:** Around 51.2cm long from head to heel. The baby is squashed but should still move in their usual pattern; movements should not slow down or stop, and if they do it could be an important sign that something is wrong. Contact the midwife or maternity unit straight away; someone answers 24 hours a day. Size: **around 51.2cm, head to heel**. Weight: not stated. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-40/ . Tommy's: babies "should not move less in late pregnancy". Source: https://www.tommys.org/pregnancy-information/im-pregnant/pregnancy-week-by-week/40-weeks-pregnant

**Her:** Lots of painless Braxton Hicks; real contractions hurt as the bump tightens and ease as it relaxes. The first stage of labour lasts 6 to 12 hours (less if she has had children before). There are no proven ways to safely bring on labour at home. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-40/ . **Due:** an antenatal appointment this week if this is her first baby: blood pressure, bump measurement, urine (pre-eclampsia checks). Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-40/

**How he can help:**
- Rehearse the NHS labour tips: massage, positions (she can put her arms around your neck and lean on you), breathing in through the nose and out through the mouth, healthy snacks such as a banana. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-40/
- Skip the curry and old wives' tales; get advice from the midwife before trying anything to start labour. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-40/
- Phone charged, car fuelled, bags in the car (report 01, Part B9).

**Watch for:** Movements (above and alert 4). If her waters break and labour does not start within 24 hours, an induction is offered because of the infection risk. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/signs-that-labour-has-begun/

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** a small cabin suitcase (around 50 to 55cm tall).

### Week 41
**Baby:** No length given. "The average baby is now around 3 to 4kg." The fast growth period is over and the baby should be chubby and mature enough to survive outside the womb without help. Overdue babies often have red, dry, peeling skin from losing their vernix; the NHS says don't moisturise it, as it peels off by itself after a few days. Size: **length not stated; weight around 3 to 4kg (average)**. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-41/ . **Her:** She may feel very frustrated. NHS: it is usually safe to go up to a week past the due date, but from 41 weeks the risks, though still very small, start to increase (needing a caesarean, the baby needing neonatal care, stillbirth and neonatal death). Options may include extra monitoring or an induction, usually with a membrane sweep first. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-41/ . **Due:** 41-week appointment to discuss choices if still pregnant after 41 weeks. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ . Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/inducing-labour/

**How he can help:**
- Go to the 41-week appointment and help her get her questions answered; NHS: "ask as many questions as you like". Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-41/
- Plan the induction logistics: it happens in a hospital maternity unit and usually takes many hours. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/inducing-labour/
- Keep her company and keep visitors and calls away (editorial).

**Watch for:** Movements (alert 4), waters and labour signs (alert 8), and the week 39 emergency list. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-39/

**Size comparison (editorial suggestion):** by weight, a 6-pint bottle of milk (about 3.5kg).

## Verification notes for the app team

1. **No NHS weights.** The NHS week pages give a length for every week from 4 to 40 but no weight until week 41 ("around 3 to 4kg"). Week 31 says "size and weight of a coconut" with no number; week 32 gives a 1kg fat gain, not a total weight. The only other weights come from Tommy's (week 17: 150g; week 34: 2.5kg) and NHS inform, which is NHS Scotland (NHS 24), not nhs.uk (weeks 21 to 22: 500g; 35: 2.3kg; 36: "probably" 2.7kg; 37 to 40: about 14g a day). Commercial sites publish a weight for every week, but they were deliberately not used. If the app needs a weekly weight, choose one source, flag it as secondary, and avoid mixing sources (see note 3).
2. **Measurement switch.** The NHS says head to heel "now" at week 20. Weeks 21 and 22 say "head to toe" and week 23 onwards "head to heel", which mean the same thing. The NHS does not state the measurement type for weeks 4, 5, 6, 8 and 13; weeks 7, 9 to 12 and 14 to 19 say "head to bottom". Weeks 4 and 5 are both given as about 2mm. The 15.3cm to 25.6cm jump at week 20 is a change of method; the app should explain it.
3. **Weights conflict between sources.** Tommy's gives 2.5kg at 34 weeks, while NHS inform gives 2.3kg at 35 weeks and 2.7kg at 36 weeks, so the curve goes down and up again if you mix sources. Treat all weights as rough averages.
4. **Length differences (small).** Tommy's vs NHS: week 17 13cm vs 12cm; 21 27cm vs 26.7cm; 26 35cm vs 35.6cm; 30 40cm vs 39.9cm; 35 46cm vs 46.2cm; 37 "more than 48cm" vs 48.6cm; 38 "nearly 50cm" vs 49.8cm. NHS inform uses ranges of weeks: about 2.5cm head to bottom at week 9 (NHS: 22mm); 4cm at weeks 10 to 11; 7cm at 13 to 14; 13cm at 17 to 18; 25.5cm at 20; 40cm at 29 to 30; 42cm at 31 to 32.
5. **First heartbeat timing.** The NHS says week 5 ("will beat for the first time around now"), Tommy's says around week 6, and NHS inform says "by week 7". **Heart rate:** NHS week 10 says 180bpm, but NHS week 28 says about 170bpm at weeks 9 and 10. This is an inconsistency within the NHS's own pages; avoid quoting an exact early bpm, or quote one figure with its source.
6. **Eyes.** NHS week 5 says the eyes stay closed until around 28 weeks; NHS and Tommy's week 26 say they open for the first time around now; NHS inform (27 to 28) says the baby might begin to open and close their eyes. App wording: "around 26 to 28 weeks".
7. **Hearing.** NHS week 15 says the baby "will start hearing"; NHS inform says at 13 to 14 weeks the ears have developed "but they can't hear yet", and that at 21 to 22 weeks the baby can hear inside and outside sounds. Tommy's week 15 says "might". App wording: "starting to hear from around 15 to 18 weeks".
8. **Appointment windows differ between NHS pages and NICE.** Booking: NHS 8 to 12 weeks; NICE "by 10+0 weeks"; Tommy's (partner page) 8 to 10 weeks. Dating scan: NHS week pages "8 to 14 weeks"; NHS 12-week scan page "around 10 to 14 weeks"; NHS appointments page "11 to 14"; NICE 11+2 to 14+1. Anomaly scan: NHS week 18 "around 18 to 20"; NHS 20-week scan page 18 to 21 (up to 23 in some cases); NICE 18+0 to 20+6. Report 01 uses 8 to 12, 11 to 14 and 18 to 21, which is fine; keep using those.
9. **40-week appointment.** The NHS week 40 page says it is for a first baby. The NHS appointments page lists a 40-week appointment without that qualifier. NICE plans 10 appointments for a first baby and 7 for later pregnancies, which only adds up if 40 weeks is first-baby only. The app should treat it as first-baby only, and report 01's table could note this.
10. **Sleep on side.** NHS England pages and NICE say from 28 weeks. NHS inform (Scotland) says "From 24 weeks you should start going to sleep lying on your side." Use 28 weeks (NHS/NICE) as the main rule, and optionally note that some NHS boards say 24.
11. **First movements.** NHS movements page: 16 to 24 weeks (after 20 for many first-timers). NHS week 17: many women first notice them between 18 and 24 weeks. RCOG: most at 18 to 20 weeks. Tommy's: from 16 weeks if she has been pregnant before. All agree on telling the midwife if nothing has been felt by 24 weeks.
12. **"Viable" at 24 weeks.** The NHS week 24 page uses "viable". This is sensitive for families who have lost a baby; prefer the NHS premature birth page wording ("possible for a baby to survive if born around 24 weeks onwards"). NHS week 7 says miscarriage is loss "in the first 23 weeks"; the NHS miscarriage page says "before 24 weeks" (https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/miscarriage/). Use "before 24 weeks".
13. **Vernix and lanugo at week 29.** The NHS week 29 sentence is ambiguous about whether the vernix, the downy hair, or both start to disappear. Tommy's (week 29) and NHS week 39 both say the vernix is still there late on, so the app should say "the downy hair starts to go; the creamy vernix coating may still be there at birth".
14. **The RCOG movements leaflet** is based on its 2011 Green-top Guideline 57, so it is older, but its advice matches the current NHS and Tommy's pages. The NHS ectopic symptoms page was last reviewed in August 2022, and its review was due in August 2025 (overdue at the time of this pass).
15. **Dated NHS content to avoid copying.** Weeks 27, 28 and 32 link to RCOG COVID-19 guidance, and week 33 mentions keeping change "in case you need to use the payphone". Don't carry these into the app.
16. **URL changes (matters for the content lint).** NHS "Start for Life" is now "Best Start in Life". Old /start-for-life/ URLs redirect to /best-start-in-life/, and https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/ redirects to the guide index. The report 01 URL https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-appointments/ now redirects to https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ . Several NHS symptom pages have moved from /pregnancy/related-conditions/... to /pregnancy/common-symptoms/... or /pregnancy/complications/... . This report cites the final (post-redirect) URLs.
17. **Review dates.** The NHS week-by-week pages show no review date. Tommy's week pages were last reviewed 24 June 2025 (next review 24 June 2028). NHS inform's week-to-week page was last updated 29 September 2026. Tommy's has no 41-week page (404), so week 41 relies on the NHS only.
18. **Report 01 follow-up.** NHS week 20 confirms the MAT B1 is issued from 20 weeks (report 01, Verification note 2, listed MAT B1 timing as unconfirmed).
19. **Size comparisons** are editorial. Object dimensions are typical, approximate values from general knowledge, not taken from any source, and products vary (plates, backpacks, laptops, monitors, pillows). Branded objects (Rubik's Cube, Game Boy, PS5, iPad) may need a trademark check before use. No secondary or commercial source was used for any clinical fact in this report.
20. **"How he can help"** items are practical suggestions built on the cited source for that week. Where a bullet has no source, it is marked "(editorial)" or points to report 01.
