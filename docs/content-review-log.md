## Engine behaviour (GoNoGoEngine)

How the engine turns NHS wording into rules. The code is in `Sources/MissionCore/Engine/`; every rule below has a test, and the scripted labours in `Tests/MissionCoreTests/Fixtures/` show each one end to end.

| # | Rule | Notes | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Frequency is start to start; duration is start to end. | Matches how NHS and ACOG describe timing. | Open |
| E2 | Statistics use the contractions in the last hour (fixed; no setting changes it), and need at least 4. The call rule is judged on the current rate: the contractions in the last 20 minutes, or the last 4 if there are fewer. | Fewer than 4 shows "not enough recent contractions". The current rate means a labour that speeds up is judged on how it is now, not on its slower start. | Open |
| E3 | Distances are measured in the run's pace: the upper quartile of its gaps in the hour before its last start, so two close contractions don't make the pace look short and it only changes when he taps. No contraction for 3× the pace: the app asks "Are they still coming?" and keeps the advice. No contraction for 5×: the pattern is stale and the 15-minute hold (E11) starts. A gap over 5× starts a new run. A gap within 0.2 of 2× to 5× the run's typical interval counts as missed taps only if the run is otherwise regular, the next gap is back on rhythm, and such gaps are at most a quarter of the run (so coupled contractions are never "missed taps"); it's said on the call as "possibly N not logged". Until the next contraction confirms the rhythm, the advice is held with the "paused" note. Starts more than a minute in the future are bad timestamps: left out of every statistic and flagged to fix. | B14. Three missed taps used to restart the hour and drop "Time to call". A slowdown, an ordinary longer gap or an irregular run is never filled in, so missing data can't make a pattern look quicker or more regular than it was. | Open |
| E4 | The call rate is met when any of these hold: at least three in four of the recent intervals (counted) are at or under the advice interval; their median is at or under it and three in four are at or under 1.35× it (T5); or, over at least 6 intervals of the run in the last hour, the average is at or under it and no gap is over twice it, which covers coupled contractions. The same tests at 1.5× give "Keep timing". Frequent but uneven contractions, or ones met on the average, read "Frequent, every 1 min to 7 min". | B14. Every test is one-sided: a property test checks that shortening any interval never fails the rule, another that a labour that only speeds up never gets later advice, and another that a looser threshold never gives later advice than a tighter one. Over a whole log, shortening one gap can still give later advice in three known ways: a hold isn't reached yet because time is compressed; an older, longer gap slides into the 20-minute window; or a run stops being regular enough for a missed-tap gap to be filled in. Coupled contractions were "Keep timing" for hours. **Midwife question:** is "on average every 5 minutes, no gap over 10, over 6 or more" right for coupled contractions? | Open |
| E5 | "Sustained" runs from the first contraction of the current run to the earlier of now and one typical interval of the run after the last start. It only matters when a hold is set (T2). It never shrinks without a new tap. | Silence doesn't count. A slower but unbroken lead-in counts towards the hour, which leans towards calling earlier (fixture 32). | Open |

| E7 | A contraction over 2 minutes in the last hour means call now. | | Open |

| E9 | Under 37+0 weeks, any labour session means call now. Gestational age is fixed when labour mode starts. | E10 | Red flags hold until the partner turns them off; turning one off takes effect at once. | Undo is the fix for a mis-tap. | Open |
| E11 | Contraction-driven advice rises immediately but only drops after 15 minutes of lower readings, showing "The pattern has slowed or paused. This advice stays up for 15 minutes in case it picks up again." It drops to the highest level seen in those 15 minutes. While new contractions are being logged but there are still too few to measure, the hold doesn't run down. | **Question for Phase 3:** corrections (editing, deleting or undoing a contraction) are also held for 15 minutes, so fixing a mis-tap that triggered "call now" keeps it up with the easing note. Safe, but possibly annoying. The old wording "Contractions have eased" was untrue in that case. | Open |
| E12 | When a hold is set, second or later babies, a previous fast labour, or a journey over 30 minutes need the pattern for 30 minutes at most (see T6). | | Open |

| E15 | The US profile says "your provider or labor and delivery unit" and 911. | | Open |

| E17 | "They've stopped" (an event in the log) drops non-urgent advice at once, with no 15-minute hold. If the next contraction comes on rhythm anyway (within 3× the pace of the last one), it was a mis-tap or a short pause and is cancelled. Otherwise the next contraction starts a new run, which needs 4 contractions and has to meet the call point again; the call-point time (E20) is kept until then, in case it was a mis-tap. It never clears an urgent rule (over 2 minutes, 6 in 10) or a red flag. A "they've stopped" stamped in the future is ignored. | B14. | Open |
| E18 | A tap on a contraction that has been open longer than 5 minutes asks "Did it just end, or is this a new one?" rather than guessing.
